Roof Leak Water Damage Help in Smyrna

A roof leak rarely starts where the stain appears. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. A ceiling stain tends to arrive late, well after the space overhead first got wet. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. Our job is the inside of the house: pulling out soaked insulation, drying the framing and drywall, treating for mold and putting the ceiling back together. Should the leak still be active, our crew can cover that section with a tarp for now, or time our work with the roofing contractor you pick so nothing gets sealed over an open leak.

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsIf water is dripping through a light fixture, a ceiling fan or near an outlet, switch off that circuit at the breaker and leave the fixture alone.
  2. Catch the DripsPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneDo not climb onto a wet roof or crawl through a dark attic. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Document What You SeeTake photos and video of the ceiling, the attic and any damaged belongings before you move or throw anything away, then call us to start drying.

Insurance

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. A leak put down to an old, worn or neglected roof is usually denied, and fixing the roof may be treated separately from the interior work. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • The length of time the leak went unnoticed, plus whether mold has started.
  • Damage beyond the ceiling, such as wet floors, wall cavities or furniture.
  • How easy the attic is to reach, how high the ceilings are, and if the roof needs a tarp before work begins.
  • The amount of ceiling and wall drywall that needs to be cut out.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Missing or broken shingles, often after high wind, with bare underlayment beneath.
  • Gutters packed with leaves, which push water back beneath the first row of shingles.
  • Roof valleys where worn metal or piled debris lets water get under the covering.
  • Storm impact, such as hail or a tree branch, breaking through the shingles.
  • Flashing that has rusted, lifted or pulled away around chimneys, vents and walls.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Rafters and Decking

Repeated wetting makes decking, trusses and rafters turn dark, swell up and go soft. Decay here means less strength in what carries the roof.

Mold in the Attic

Mold does well in an attic that is warm, dark and full of damp wood and drywall paper. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours and spread across the underside of the decking.

Drywall Ceilings

A ceiling panel left damp will discolor, bow and weaken. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Lights and Wiring

Because fixtures are holes in the drywall, a leak often shows up at a light or fan first. Moisture in a fixture can cause a short and later corrosion, which is why it needs inspection before use.
