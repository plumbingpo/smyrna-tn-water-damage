Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Smyrna

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. A refrigerator ice maker line is a thin tube that can drip behind the unit for months. Cleanup is different for each of them.

For homeowners in Smyrna, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the Machine DownStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.
The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • A kinked or cracked ice maker line, or one whose fitting has worked loose.
  • Worn dishwasher door gaskets, cracked drain hoses or a failed pump seal.
  • The drain hose of the washer pulling free of the standpipe, or the standpipe backing up from a clog.
  • Installation mistakes like a loose supply line connection or a dishwasher that does not sit level.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Mold Behind Appliances

Behind a refrigerator or a dishwasher it is warm and dark, and nobody checks there often. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Kitchen Cabinets

Dishwasher leaks soak cabinet sides and toe kicks from underneath. Swelling and separation follow, and for weeks the soaked cabinet boxes may trap moisture against the wall.

Rooms Next Door

For hours, a failed washer hose can keep releasing water. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Flooring and Subfloor

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Cost

How the Quote Is Built

  • Any mold growth, because it must be dealt with ahead of drying.
  • Duration of the leak, particularly a hidden drip that went unnoticed behind the machine.
  • Removal of cabinets or flooring, compared with drying them in place when that is possible.
  • How many rooms the water reached, including any ceiling below an upstairs laundry.

What We Do

Our Appliance Leak Water Damage Process

1

Pull and Inspect

The appliance is disconnected and moved, and the supply is confirmed off. Then thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went below flooring, under the cabinets and into the walls nearby.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is extracted from floors and carpet. Where water is trapped under cabinets or floating floors, we remove toe kicks or lift boards to reach it.

Dry in Place

Dehumidifiers and air movers are set up, and when it is possible, directed airflow dries hardwood and cabinets where they sit so nothing has to be torn out.

4

Clean and Confirm

We apply antimicrobial treatment wherever discharge from the washer or the dishwasher made contact. Final moisture readings are taken before we return the appliance, so it never sits on a damp floor.
