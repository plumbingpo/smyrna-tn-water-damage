Bathroom Water Damage: What Smyrna Homeowners Should Know

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. In Smyrna, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Signs to Look For

  • Tiles near the tub that have loosened, shift when stepped on, or sound hollow
  • Slight rocking when you sit on the toilet, or a dark ring on the floor around it
  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
  • A brown ring or sagging spot on the ceiling directly below the bathroom
  • Vinyl flooring that curls at the edges or has discolored near the toilet or tub
  • Hours after anyone showered, the grout is still dark with moisture
Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Slow Vanity Drips

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. First the bottom of the cabinet swells, and then the wall behind and the subfloor get wet.

Mold Behind Tile

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

Trapped Steam

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

A Failed Shower Pan

With a crack in the liner or pan, water escapes below the shower floor at each use. Up top everything looks fine, yet the framing underneath never dries.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few days

Water Slips Through

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.

Our Process

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Open What Is Needed

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Cavities

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.
