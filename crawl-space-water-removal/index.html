Crawl Space Water Removal Help in Smyrna

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

Warning Signs

  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
  • Tide lines or white mineral crust on the piers and foundation walls
  • A musty, earthy smell that is strongest near the floor vents
  • Fiberglass insulation sagging or hanging down between the floor joists
  • Wood flooring on the first level that has begun to curl up along the seams
  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Pump Out Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Final Drying and Checks

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

Air From Below

Because air naturally moves upward from under the home, spores and stale odors in a wet crawl space reach your living areas too.

Shifting Piers and Supports

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. Doors that stick and floors that slope upstairs are possible signs.

Weakened Floor Framing

The first soft wood is usually hidden, up where joists meet the subfloor or back at the sill plate. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First day

Water Pools Below

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

Past one week

Rot Sets In

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Joists and sill plates soften, fasteners rust and the damp space attracts termites and other pests.
