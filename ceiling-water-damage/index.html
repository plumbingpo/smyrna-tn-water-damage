Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Smyrna

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. By the time the stain appears, water has already run along a joist, soaked the insulation and pooled on the back of the drywall. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. A sagging ceiling is a different matter. Do not stand beneath it, and get help on the way.

What It Looks Like

  • Patches of spotty discoloration that look like mold on the ceiling
  • New fine cracks that radiate from a discolored patch
  • Blistered, bubbling or flaking paint on the ceiling
  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure
  • One room that smells damp even though nothing looks wet
  • Water dripping from a light fixture, vent or smoke detector
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold Above You

Even when the ceiling feels dry to the touch, the batts and the unseen face of the drywall remain damp, giving mold a hidden place to spread.

Electricity Overhead

The damp area usually includes fan mounts, recessed fixtures and wiring boxes. A short is possible when water enters a light, which is why the breaker should be shut off.

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

Water follows joists, pipes and ducts, so it can drip several feet from where it entered. Cutting in just under the mark may leave damp areas elsewhere untouched.

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Asbestos was used in certain older plasters and popcorn finishes. If your home has one, it should be tested before anyone scrapes, cuts or tears out a damaged area.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Source

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. Repairs are pointless until that leak is shut down.

2

Secure the Room

Furnishings are protected or relocated, and electricity to affected lights is turned off. Where water is trapped overhead, it gets drained under control into containers.

3

Open and Dry

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. The joists and nearby ceiling are dried with air movers plus a dehumidifier, and antimicrobial goes on wherever the water was dirty.

4

Repair and Finish

After the meter shows normal levels, new insulation and drywall go up, joints are taped and finished, the texture is matched and a stain blocking primer goes on ahead of paint.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

Hours 24 to 48

Marks Start Showing

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Days 3 through 7

Sagging Sets In

The panel turns soft and starts slipping off its fasteners. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

After a week

Risk of Collapse

Parts of the ceiling can come down along with the wet insulation resting on them. Mold is established in the cavity and the repair now covers a much larger area.
